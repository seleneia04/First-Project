Solar System

consists of the Sun and everything bound to it by gravity: eight planets, dozens of moons, and millions of smaller bodies like asteroids and comets. It formed about 4.6 billion years ago from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust.

Eight very different planets

The planets in our solar system also come in different sizes. There are small planets, like Mercury, barely one and a half times larger than our Moon, and giant planets, the biggest being Jupiter, more massive and voluminous than the seven others put together. All of them circle the Sun, taking more or less longer to do so according to how far they are from our star. Mercury, the closest, takes just three months, while Neptune, the furthest, takes 165 years. The solar system also comprises five dwarf planets and a multitude of smaller wandering asteroids and comets.

Some years ago, there were nine planets in our solar system. But in 2006, Pluto, the most distant, had its status downgraded to dwarf planet, too small to exert its dominance and clear the surrounding neighbourhood, that is, to attract other small bodies in its orbital vicinity. However, there may still be a ninth planet lurking somewhere on the edges of the solar system. Scientists have observed some odd quirks in asteroid trajectories that might be due to the gravitational pull of a planet ten times more massive than Earth . But its thought to be so far away that spotting it will be hard. Astronomers are still searching where best to point their telescopes.

The Rocky Inner Planets

Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars are terrestrial planets with solid surfaces. They differ in atmosphere, climate, and potential for hosting life, with Earth being the only planet known to support life.

The Gas Giants

Jupiter and Saturn dominate with their immense size, thick atmospheres, and extensive moon systems. Jupiter's Great Red Spot and Saturn's iconic rings make them particularly fascinating.

The Ice Giants

Uranus and Neptune, composed mainly of ices and gases, are mysterious worlds with striking features like Neptune's deep blue colour and Uranus's tilted axis.
